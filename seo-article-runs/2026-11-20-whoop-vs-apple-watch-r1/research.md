## 4. Fit verdict

**`article_fit`, confidence medium.**

1. *Does the SERP reward an article?* Yes. Six of the organic rows are comparison articles; forums and video take the rest.
2. *Can this site produce the artifact credibly?* Partly. Every ranking article is first-person hands-on, and the publisher has tested neither device. A documentation-and-evidence comparison is a different artifact from the ones ranking. It is credible only if it says so plainly and wins on things hands-on reviews did not do: dated vendor facts, like-for-like buying fields, primary studies with their limits.
3. *Click opportunity?* An AI Overview and a megathread sit above most articles. Traffic estimates on the ranking pages (161 to 1,929) show clicks still reach articles.
4. *Business purpose?* Clear: the reader with an Apple Watch asking what closes the recovery-score gap is the app's buyer. This is also the conflict of interest the article must disclose.
5. *Information gain?* Real and checkable: (a) none of the three selected pages covers Series 12, Ultra 4 or Apple's Readiness app, and CNET's tier table is out of date; (b) no page completes the same buying fields for both sides; (c) no page cites primary accuracy evidence, and Apple published a head-to-head heart-rate study including Whoop 5 in September 2026 (E20); (d) no page says what Whoop does and does not write to Apple Health (E7).
6. *Realistic peers?* Yes: DR 28, DR 11 and DR 38 pages rank 9 to 11; KD 3.

Main risk: Google may be rewarding lived experience here, and this article has none. Second risk: the freshness advantage is temporary; GGR has already promised a Series 12 update.

## 6. Archetype recommendation

**`X vs Y`, confidence high.**

- *For:* two named options; all six ranking articles are X vs Y pages with criteria and persona verdicts; the reader task needs criteria parity, total cost, switching friction and audience-specific verdicts, which is the X vs Y contract.
- *Against:* the publisher's interest pulls toward a third option (keep the watch, add an app), which could turn the piece into an Alternatives page. ### Candidate angles (none approved; the user decides at Checkpoint A)

**Does the intake's one-line direction survive?** ("what Whoop gives that an Apple Watch does not out of the box, and how far an app closes the gap"). Only in a modified form.

- *Evidence for it:* PAA asks "What can a WHOOP do that an Apple Watch can't?". The first organic result makes this exact argument (C1), and C4 ends on it, so the demand is real.
- *Evidence against it as written:*
  1. "Out of the box" now has two answers. Apple's Readiness app gives a 0 to 10 daily score from activity, vitals (including HRV) and sleep, but only on Series 12 and Ultra 4 (E12). The SE 3 has no Readiness app and no HRV in Vitals (E13, E9). The gap depends on which watch the reader has.
  2. It is not new. Position 2 already says it, with 60 days of use behind it. Repeating it without experience is a weaker copy of the top result.
  3. "How far an app closes the gap" cannot be measured without testing. It can only be specified: what any Apple Health app cannot change (battery and overnight wear, a screenless band, wear positions, Android, Whoop's all-day Strain with muscular load) and what an app can add (a daily score, guidance). The publisher's own app has no strain score and no journal correlation engine (product facts), so it does not close the strain half.
  4. If the app is the organising idea, the article leads with the publisher's interest. The intake requires a fair comparison first.

**Angle A (recommended): "It depends which Apple Watch you have."** A decision-first comparison organised by the reader's starting point. Series 12 or Ultra 4: Apple now gives a readiness score, so the question is what a $199 to $359 a year membership still adds (14+ day battery, an all-day Strain target, the Journal, a screenless band, non-wrist wear, Android). SE 3 or an older watch: no Apple Readiness app, so the choice is an app, a newer watch, or a Whoop. Neither device yet: cost over one to three years and the job each is built for. Both: what actually flows between them.
- *For:* it is the one thing the ranking pages have not caught up with (C1, C2 predate it; C3 defers it); every claim is vendor-verifiable (E8 to E14); it matches the model-specific PAA question; it keeps the intake's direction but puts Apple's own feature before any app; it tells some readers to buy a Whoop.
- *Against:* overlaps the sibling article on older watches, so the model detail must stay to a sentence or two; nobody can say from evidence whether Apple's Readiness score or Whoop's Recovery is the better score (Unresolved, section 7); the freshness edge will close when the reviewers update.

**Angle B (runner-up): "What you actually pay, and what each tier or model unlocks."** Cost of ownership over one, two and three years, with the feature gates on each side (Whoop One has no Health Monitor or Stress Monitor; ECG needs Whoop Life or Apple's Series 12 or Ultra 4; Readiness needs Series 12 or Ultra 4), plus the optional app subscription.
- *For:* the strongest fully verified material in the packet (E1 to E5, E8, K1); every competitor covers price but none completes it, and one has it wrong; calculation is original and checkable.
- *Against:* price is one criterion, not the whole decision; promotional prices move (Whoop's "Limited Time", Apple's Black Friday offer); how long an Apple Watch lasts before replacement is an assumption, not a fact; the recovery question the reader came with becomes secondary.

**Angle C (the intake's direction, unchanged).** For and against as listed above. Not recommended as the frame; its content survives inside Angle A as one section.

### Accuracy (primary studies; file `primary-studies.md`, Apple PDF in `apple-hr-accuracy-study-2026.md`)

- **E18** `source`, study. Miller, Sargent, Roach, Sensors 2022, https://doi.org/10.3390/s22166317 (open access, full text read through Europe PMC). Devices: **Apple Watch Series 6** and **WHOOP 3.0** among six; 53 healthy adults, one laboratory night, polysomnography and ECG. Multi-state sleep agreement "53% (κ = 0.20) for Apple Watch ... 60% (κ = 0.44) for WHOOP". Overnight HRV against ECG: Apple Watch "intraclass correlation was 0.67", WHOOP "intraclass correlation was 0.99". Heart rate ICC 0.96 and 0.99. Support partial for any present-day claim. Not a like-for-like test of the two HRV pipelines.
