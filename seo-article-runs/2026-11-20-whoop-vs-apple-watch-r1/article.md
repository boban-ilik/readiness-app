Whoop's pitch is a daily recovery score. Apple now has one too, on two of its three current watches: Apple lists [its Readiness app](https://support.apple.com/en-us/128112) for the Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4, and its SE 3 product page shows a dash for the app. So the answer depends on which Apple Watch you have, or would buy.

With a Series 12 or Ultra 4 you already have a daily score, so a Whoop has to earn its yearly fee with what it documents beyond that. With an SE 3 or an older watch, a Whoop is one of three routes to a score. On Android, an Apple Watch is not an option.

The method: both companies' own pages, read on 20 November 2026, plus published studies. This site has tested neither device. Its publisher makes an iPhone app in this category, covered in one labelled section.

## What you are actually comparing: a membership and a watch

## Whoop Recovery and Strain, and Apple's Readiness: what each score is

Both scores compare your body's signals with your own recent normal. HRV feeds both, and both companies read a value above your usual as a good sign.

Whoop splits the job in two: [Recovery](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/WHOOP-Recovery?language=en_US), a morning percentage for how prepared your body is, and [Strain](https://support.whoop.com/s/article/WHOOP-Strain?language=en_US), a separate score for exertion. Apple's Readiness app gives one score, drawing on Vitals (overnight metrics tracked against your typical range) and training load.

| | Whoop (all three tiers) | Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Daily score | Recovery, a percentage in three bands: green at 67% or above, yellow from 34% to 66%, red at 33% or below. Shown each morning | Apple's Readiness score, 0 to 10 in four classes: Recover (0 to 1), Pace Yourself (2 to 4), Ready (5 to 7), Go for it (8 to 10). Updates through the day |
| Inputs and baseline | HRV and resting heart rate (the two biggest contributors, per Whoop), respiratory rate, sleep against need, sleep stages, skin temperature, blood oxygen and cycle phase. HRV is compared with a 30-day baseline | Per Apple's guide: activity (active calories, effort ratings, training load), vitals (overnight heart rate, respiratory rate, wrist temperature, HRV and, where available, blood oxygen; daytime heart rate) and sleep score. Vitals are compared with a baseline that needs 7 of the past 49 nights |
| Exertion measure | Strain, 0 to 21, for the whole day and for each activity, from cardiovascular and muscular load, with a daily Optimal Strain target | Training load, from workouts: the last 7 days against the previous 28 |
| How exertion meets the score | Kept apart. Whoop says Strain does not directly affect Recovery; Recovery feeds the Strain target | Folded in. Apple says more activity can lower its Readiness score, including during the day after a workout |

The fourth row is the structural difference. Which design gives the more useful number, two scores or one, is not settled: no validation of either score was captured. Nor is it settled whether Strain or training load is the better exertion measure, or how good either company's AI coaching is.

Both companies frame the score as advice: Apple calls its Readiness app a general wellness feature, and Whoop says it is not a medical diagnostic tool. Whether a daily number should change your training at all is covered in [using HRV to guide training](https://thereadiness.app/blog/hrv-training/).

## If you have a Series 12 or Ultra 4

As of 20 November 2026, Apple lists its Readiness app for the Series 12 (from $399) and the Ultra 4 (from $799), so you already have a daily score. A Whoop membership, at $199 to $359 a year, has to earn its place with what Whoop documents beyond that score:

- **Battery.** "14+ day" by Whoop's figure, against Apple's up to 24 hours (Series 12) and up to 50 (Ultra 4).
- **All-day Strain.** A whole-day score with a daily target, counting muscular load as well as heart rate. Apple's training load is built from workouts.
- **No screen.** Whoop left the watch face out on purpose.
- **Also listed.** Journal and Behavior Impact on every tier, and wear at other validated body locations, such as the bicep, for the WHOOP 5.0 and MG. Both are noted as listed features, nothing more.

For "what can a Whoop do that an Apple Watch can't?", that list is the documented answer: it is what Whoop's pages state and Apple's captured pages do not, and it is not a tested limit of the watch. If none of it solves a problem you actually have, stay put. Nothing in the evidence says a second score is a better one.

Against the Ultra 4 the battery gap is narrower, and Apple states up to 84 hours in Low Power Mode. The Ultra 4 also has the ECG app and Emergency SOS via satellite, and Whoop Life, the tier with ECG, passes its $799 price at the third annual payment ($1,077).

## If you have an SE 3 or an older Apple Watch

Apple lists its Readiness app for the Series 12 and Ultra 4, and its SE 3 product page shows a dash for the app. watchOS 27 runs on Series 9 and later and Ultra 2 and later, but Apple notes that not every feature reaches every device; the detail is in [which Apple Watch models get Apple's Readiness app](https://thereadiness.app/blog/apple-watch-readiness-older-watches/).

On the SE 3, Apple's Vitals app comes without HRV, though Apple's SE 3 page still lists HRV among the watch's features. What is missing is Apple's Readiness app. At prices read on 20 November 2026, there are three routes to a daily recovery score:

1. **A morning score and nothing else: an app.** The four in the next section have annual plans from $29.99 to $99.99, and two say the score itself is free.
2. **Apple's own score: a Series 12,** from $399 once. Over an SE 3 it also adds the ECG app, hypertension notifications and HRV in Vitals.
3. **Multi-day battery, no screen and all-day Strain: a Whoop.** Whoop One is $199 a year, $597 over three years, on top of the watch you own.

"Whoop or Apple Watch SE?" follows from the same facts. For a built-in daily recovery score, buy the Whoop: every tier has Recovery and the SE 3 does not have Apple's Readiness app. For a watch first, the SE 3 is $249 once against $199 a year, with the score coming from an app.

## If you own neither yet

On Android, buy a Whoop: Apple says its watch is compatible with iPhone and nothing else, and Whoop has an Android app.

On iPhone, choose by the job, at prices read on 20 November 2026.

- **Buy a Whoop** if a daily recovery read is the main job and you want a band rated for two weeks or more between charges. Whoop One, at $199 a year, includes Recovery and Strain. Budget $597 over three years.
- **Buy an Apple Watch** if you want a watch: a screen, GPS, a cellular option and emergency features. Choose the Series 12 (from $399) over the SE 3 (from $249) if you want Apple's Readiness app built in.

Whoop refunds in full, minus shipping, within 30 days of receipt. On its 1-month Peak trial (availability Unknown), you must start the return inside the trial month to avoid a charge, and a $110 restocking fee applies to hardware not returned. Apple's trial and return terms were not captured, so they are Unknown and no edge is called on returns.

## If you already wear a Whoop, or want to wear both

If you are weighing a switch, the model matters again: a Series 12 or Ultra 4 has its own daily score, shown once the watch has been worn to bed for at least 7 nights, and an SE 3 does not. Past the 30-day return window, a membership runs to the end of its commitment period, so you can read Apple's score beside Recovery until then. Once it ends, Whoop stops collecting new data. Neither score is shown to be the better one, so the case for switching rests on cost and on wanting a watch, not on the score.

If you want both, the data flow is documented. Whoop's Apple Health article, dated 16 April 2026, lists what Whoop writes to Apple Health (workouts, sleep, resting heart rate, respiratory rate and blood oxygen among them) and what it reads back (workouts and routes among them). HRV is the exception: Whoop does not export it. Its stated reason, as of that date, is that Apple Health uses SDNN for HRV while Whoop calculates RMSSD, a difference covered in [what each brand reports as HRV](https://thereadiness.app/blog/what-is-a-good-hrv/).

At regular prices read on 20 November 2026, a Series 12 plus Whoop One comes to $598 after one year and $996 after three. Wearing both is documented and priced; whether two devices read better than one is not something the evidence covers.

## What the accuracy studies can and cannot settle

The three studies used here each put a Whoop and an Apple Watch through the same test. None is an independent test of today's hardware.

[Miller, Sargent and Roach (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22166317) tested an Apple Watch Series 6 and a WHOOP 3.0 on 53 healthy adults for one night in a sleep laboratory. Sleep-stage agreement with polysomnography, the laboratory reference, measured as kappa (0 is chance, 1 is perfect), was 0.20 for the Apple Watch and 0.44 for the Whoop. For overnight HRV against ECG, the intraclass correlation was 0.67 for the Apple Watch and 0.99 for the Whoop. Two caveats: the Apple Watch data came through a third-party app in 5-minute blocks, and Whoop supplied its data to the researchers while Apple did not.

[Schyvens and colleagues (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleepadvances/zpaf021) tested an Apple Watch Series 8 and a Whoop 4.0 on 62 adults for one night. Kappa was 0.53 for the Apple Watch and 0.37 for the Whoop: the opposite direction. Each participant wore two to four of the study's six devices, so the two were not necessarily worn by the same sleepers.

[Apple's own heart-rate study](https://www.apple.com/health/pdf/Heart_Rate_Accuracy_Study_2026.pdf), dated September 2026, compared a Series 12 with five other wrist devices, a WHOOP 5 among them, against a chest strap. Apple reports higher accuracy for its watch than for every other device tested, at statistical significance on both of its error measures, in the overall workout and daily-living results. The limits: it is Apple's study of its own product, with Apple managing three of the five test sites and a contract research organisation the other two; it is not peer reviewed; it covers heart rate and not HRV, sleep or readiness; and its methods table shows the two streams collected differently, the WHOOP 5 over Bluetooth at 1-second intervals and the Apple Watch through an internal logging app at 5-second intervals.

So accuracy stays out of every recommendation above. The sleep studies disagree, the HRV comparison was not like for like, and the heart-rate result for current hardware is one company's study of its own product. The research for this article found no independent test of the WHOOP 5.0, WHOOP MG, Series 12 or Ultra 4.

Whichever way you go, re-read both price pages on the day you buy: Whoop's limited-time prices show no end date, and Apple's gift card offer runs for four days. If you choose Whoop, note the day the band arrives. The 30-day return window starts there.
