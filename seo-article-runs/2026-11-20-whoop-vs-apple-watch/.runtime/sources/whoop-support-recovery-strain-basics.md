## A. WHOOP Recovery

WHOOP Recovery

Sep 11, 2025

Overview

Recovery measures how well-prepared your body is to take on Strain and how effectively it returns to baseline after stressors such as exercise, illness, psychological stress, or sleep deprivation. The greater the stress, the more recovery your body requires.

High Recovery (Green, 67% or above) → Your body is primed to adapt to a higher training load.

Moderate Recovery (Yellow, 34-66%) → Your body is maintaining its baseline zones, but may be more susceptible to fatigue or stress.

Low Recovery (Red, 33% or below) → Your body may be under increased stress, and you should consider adjusting Strain or prioritizing recovery activities.

IN THIS ARTICLE: How Recovery is Calculated; Understanding WHOOP Metrics in Recovery; Sleep Performance and Recovery; Skin Temperature and Recovery; Frequently Asked Questions

How Recovery is Calculated

WHOOP calculates Recovery each night during Sleep and provides a score in the morning. Over time, WHOOP refines its understanding of your unique baseline and adjusts accordingly to deliver personalized insights.

Key Metrics Used in Recovery Calculations

HRV (Heart Rate Variability): Higher-than-usual HRV indicates better recovery and nervous system balance.

Resting Heart Rate (RHR): Lower RHR during sleep typically signals strong cardiovascular fitness and recovery.

Respiratory Rate (RR): Stable RR is a sign of consistent health; increases may reflect stress, fatigue, or illness.

Sleep Hours vs. Needed: Sleeping less than WHOOP estimates you need for full recovery lowers your score.

Sleep Stages (Light Sleep + Awake Time): More time in light sleep or frequent wake-ups can reduce sleep quality and impact Recovery.

Skin Temperature: Significant changes from your baseline may signal stress or illness, lowering your Recovery.

SpO₂ (Blood Oxygen Saturation): Low SpO₂ suggests reduced oxygen delivery and may decrease your score.

Menstrual Cycle Phase (if applicable): WHOOP adjusts for hormonal changes that may mimic signs of illness.

Understanding WHOOP Metrics in Recovery

1. HRV (Heart Rate Variability)

What it is: The variation in time between heartbeats, reflecting the state of your autonomic nervous system. WHOOP calculates HRV using a weighted average across your entire night of sleep, with more weight given to slow-wave sleep (SWS) and later stages of the night.

Impact on Recovery: One of the two biggest contributors to Recovery. WHOOP compares your current HRV to your 30-day baseline:

Higher-than-usual HRV → Sign of good recovery and adaptation → Boosts Recovery score.

Lower-than-usual HRV → May indicate fatigue, stress, or poor recovery → Lowers Recovery score.

2. RHR (Resting Heart Rate)

What it is: The number of heartbeats per minute while at rest, measured during sleep.

Impact on Recovery: Second major factor:

Lower RHR over time → Suggests fitness improvement or recovery → Boosts Recovery score.

Higher RHR → Could signal stress, overtraining, or illness → Lowers Recovery score.

3. Sleep Hours vs. Needed

What it is: Compares how many hours you slept vs. how many hours WHOOP estimates your body needed for full recovery.

Impact on Recovery: Sufficient or surplus sleep → Supports higher Recovery score. Insufficient sleep → Negatively impacts score.

4. Sleep Stages (Light Sleep + Awake Time)

What it is: Measures time spent in light sleep and periods awake during the night.

Impact on Recovery: More light sleep and frequent wake-ups → Less restful sleep → Lowers score. WHOOP factors this in to assess the restorative quality of your sleep.

5. Respiratory Rate

What it is: The number of breaths per minute during sleep.

Impact on Recovery: Deviations from your personal baseline (especially increases) → May signal illness or stress → Lowers score. Stable rate → Supports higher Recovery.

6. Skin Temperature

What it is: Tracks overnight skin temperature relative to your baseline.

Impact on Recovery: Significant deviations (up or down) → May indicate stress or illness → Lowers Recovery score.

7. SpO₂ (Blood Oxygen Saturation)

What it is: Measures the percentage of oxygen in your blood.

Impact on Recovery: Low SpO₂ → Suggests insufficient oxygenation → May reduce score. WHOOP also accounts for environmental factors (e.g. altitude) that can affect this signal.

8. Percentage Through Menstrual Cycle (if applicable)

What it is: Tracks where a member is in their cycle if logged.

Impact on Recovery: When WHOOP detects signs of illness, it also considers cycle phase to avoid misinterpreting normal hormonal shifts as sickness. Helps prevent false positives for illness-related Recovery dips.

Sleep Performance and Recovery

Sleep Performance is WHOOP’s core sleep metric that reflects the overall quality of your sleep each night. It’s calculated using four key factors: how much sleep you got compared to what you needed (sleep sufficiency), how consistent your sleep and wake times are, how efficiently you slept while in bed, and how much physiological stress you experienced during sleep. The score is designed to help you understand how well you’re sleeping and where you can improve to support recovery and performance.

Note for Members: WHOOP has transitioned from Monthly Performance Assessments (MPAs) to the new Month in Review (MIR). If you would like a copy of a past MPA, Membership Services can assist you.

Skin Temperature and Recovery

WHOOP tracks outer body temperature, identifying fluctuations that may indicate stress, illness, or environmental influences. Skin temperature patterns, alongside other metrics such as HRV and Respiratory Rate, can provide additional insights into physiological stressors affecting Recovery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my Recovery fluctuate even when my routine stays the same?

Recovery is influenced by multiple factors beyond your routine, including HRV, RHR, Sleep Performance, Skin Temperature & more. Even with consistency, changes in stress levels, hydration, or illness can impact your score. Look for trends over time rather than focusing on single-day fluctuations.

My Recovery is low even though I slept well—why?

Sleep is important, but Recovery is also affected by HRV, RHR, Respiratory Rate, and Skin Temperature. High Strain, dehydration, alcohol, travel, or stress can lower your score even with good sleep. Check these factors to understand your Recovery in context.

Can Recovery predict illness?

WHOOP is not a medical diagnostic tool, but elevated Respiratory Rate, HRV drops, and skin temperature changes can indicate potential physiological stress. If you notice sudden, unexplained Recovery drops, consider monitoring symptoms and consulting a healthcare provider.

What can I do to improve my Recovery score?

Maintain consistent sleep and wake times. Hydrate and fuel properly. Incorporate active recovery (light movement, stretching). Manage stress with meditation or breathwork. Avoid excess alcohol or late-night screen time.

Should I be concerned if my Skin Temperature changes?

Skin Temperature varies due to exercise, environment, hormones, or illness. A small fluctuation is normal, but if paired with a drop in HRV or an increase in Respiratory Rate, consider resting and hydrating.

WHOOP Strain

Mar 6, 2026

Overview

WHOOP Strain measures your body’s total exertion by analyzing cardiovascular load and muscular effort. Strain is a personalized, non-linear score from 0 to 21, helping you track intensity, optimize recovery, and balance training. Strain provides valuable insights to improve performance while avoiding burnout.

What is WHOOP Strain?

The Strain scale categorizes your exertion levels:

Light Strain (0-9) → Low-intensity activity, optimal for recovery.

Moderate Strain (10-13) → Supports fitness maintenance.

High Strain (14-17) → Builds endurance and strength gains.

All Out (18-21) → Maximal effort; challenging to recover from.

Strain is logarithmic, meaning it is easier to go from 0 to 4 than from 10 to 14. The score reflects the intensity of sustained effort rather than simple time-based accumulation.

Viewing Strain in the WHOOP App

Daily & Activity Strain

The Strain deep dive screen summarizes your total daily exertion. You can find it on the Home tab by tapping into the top Strain dial.

Logged activities can be viewed on the Home tab either in My Day on or under Today’s Activities when you tap the Strain dial. . From there, you can tap into any logged activity to see detailed Strain, heart rate zones, duration, and more.

Optimal Strain & WHOOP Coach

Your Optimal Strain is a daily target that helps guide your exertion to support performance and reduce the risk of overtraining.

This target is based on: Your Recovery score. The Strain you've already accumulated throughout the day. Your menstrual cycle phase, if Menstrual Cycle Insights is enabled.

The Strain target does not directly factor in past activity levels or workout types. However, WHOOP Coach can provide workout suggestions based on your preferences for type and duration.

How Strain is Calculated

WHOOP measures Strain based on cardiovascular load and muscular load. Cardiovascular load tracks your heart rate. The higher it gets and the longer it stays elevated, the more Strain you accumulate. Muscular load captures the work your muscles do, giving you a complete picture of your training load, whether you’re lifting, doing HIIT, or flowing through yoga.

WHOOP calculates the cardiovascular and muscular load to give you one complete Strain score. Regular daily activities like work, commuting, running errands, and parenting may all cause strain. So can stress, anxiety, excitement, or feeling nervous–even being a sports fan is strenuous.

WHOOP calculates your strain for the entire day, and for specific periods of exercise or activity.

1. Cardiovascular Strain

Cardiovascular load is automatically calculated using the advanced sensors in your WHOOP device. The higher your heart rate for a longer duration, the higher your strain will be.

HR Zone | Description
Zone 0 (Resting) | Minimal exertion. Rest, Sleep or very low heart rate activity.
Zone 1 (Very Light) | Warm-ups, cool-downs, and active recovery.
Zone 2 (Light) | Aerobic endurance
Zone 3 (Moderate) | Threshold training, mix of aerobic and anaerobic effort.
Zone 4 (Hard) | High-intensity workouts, athletic performance and power development.
Zone 5 (Max Effort) | Short bursts, sprint training, high cardiovascular impact.

2. Muscular Strain

Muscular Strain captures physical effort from strength-based and load-bearing movements that are not fully represented by heart rate alone.

Muscular load is the measured stress put on your muscles, bones, joints, and soft tissues during exercise. It’s the strain your musculoskeletal system takes on, separate from cardiovascular strain.

WHOOP measures muscular load using two components:

Volume: How much work you’re doing. This incorporates “effective mass,” which accounts for your body mass and the specific parts of your body that are moving. A full-body exercise like a squat produces more muscular load than a bench press because more of your body is involved in the movement.

Intensity: How hard you’re working. This measures your effort and exertion, such as how close you are to lifting your maximum, and the speed of your movement. Lifting heavier or moving faster increases intensity.

These components combine to quantify the total load on your body during strength-based activities.

Muscular load is factored into your Strain score in three ways:

Automatic estimation for strength activities: For activities like weightlifting, functional fitness, HIIT, and powerlifting, WHOOP automatically estimates muscular strain based on activity type and duration. These estimates were derived from millions of Strength Trainer sessions. No logging required — just start your activity and lift. Because this estimate is based on duration, linking your exercises gives WHOOP more to work with if intensity matters.

Link exercises after your workout: Want more accurate measurement? After your activity ends, you can link the exercises you performed. WHOOP uses your selected movements to estimate a more precise muscular load — accounting for which muscle groups were involved and the typical demands of each exercise.

Log in real-time with Strength Trainer: For the most precise tracking, log your sets, reps, and weights during your workout using Strength Trainer. WHOOP measures each rep’s speed and intensity to give you the most accurate muscular load calculation.

Yoga, Pilates, and barre are calculated automatically using research from WHOOP Labs — no logging needed.

Most wearables only track cardiovascular load, which works well for running or cycling but misses the full picture for low-impact activities like yoga and Pilates that challenge your muscles differently than cardio.

Because automatic muscular strain is estimated based on duration, a longer workout will show more muscular strain than a shorter one, even if the shorter session was more intense. For workouts where intensity matters, linking your exercises through the Strength Trainer gives WHOOP more to work with.

Using Strength Trainer provides the most detailed and individualized measurement of muscular effort.

Helps monitor accumulated fatigue over time.

Useful for preparing for competitions or recovery periods.

Max Strain & Recovery Considerations

A Strain score of 21 represents the top end of our Strain scale—indicating an exceptionally high level of cardiovascular and muscular load.

Endurance athletes (e.g., marathoners) may reach max Strain through cardio.

Strength-focused athletes can reach high Strain through muscular effort without excessive heart rate elevation.

Recovery Score should guide training intensity to prevent overtraining.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Strain feel different on some days?

Strain accumulates based on workout intensity, duration, and recovery state.

Heart rate variability, fatigue, and external factors affect your perceived effort.

Can I get a high Strain score without intense cardio?

Yes! Strength-based and load-bearing activities can lead to high Strain even without extreme heart rate elevation.

Does Strain Impact Recovery?

Not directly. Recovery is based on HRV, sleep, and physiological readiness. However, higher Strain can lead to lower next-day HRV and higher next-day RHR, indicating your body may not be fully recovered from cardiovascular or musculoskeletal load.

In short, Strain informs how much stress your body has endured, which can influence—but does not determine—your Recovery score.

Why does my Strain score seem different with WHOOP 5.0 or MG?

WHOOP 5.0 and MG devices include updated sensors and algorithm improvements that provide more accurate heart rate detection—especially during high-intensity or dynamic movement.

That means your Strain score may feel slightly higher or lower than what you were used to on WHOOP 4.0 due to improved accuracy. Over time, your Strain trends will adapt to your new device and give you a more precise picture of effort and recovery.

WHOOP Metrics

Strain

Based on the Borg Scale of Perceived Exertion, WHOOP Strain is measured on a 0 to 21 scale. The more time you spend in elevated heart rate zones, the higher your Strain. Strain does not operate on a linear scale; the higher your Strain, the harder you will have to work to accrue additional Strain.

Recovery

WHOOP Recovery is calculated using multiple metrics, including Heart Rate Variability (HRV), Resting Heart Rate (RHR), Respiratory Rate, Sleep Duration and Quality, Skin Temperature, Blood Oxygen Saturation (SpO₂), and Menstrual Cycle Phase (when applicable). Recovery scores can be influenced by lifestyle factors such as stress, alcohol, caffeine, exercise, diet, illness, and more.

Sleep

WHOOP tracks various sleep metrics, including:

Duration: Total sleep time.

Performance: Quality of sleep based on efficiency, sufficiency, sleep stress, and consistency.

Latency: Time taken to fall asleep (if using manual sleep tracking).

Sleep Stages: Time spent in Light, REM, and Deep Sleep.

Sleep sufficiency: Hours of sleep / sleep need.

Sleep consistency: Going to bed and waking up at the same time.

High Sleep stress %: Physiological stress during sleep.

Sleep efficiency: Sleep duration vs. time in bed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What position should I place my WHOOP device?

Wear WHOOP one finger-width above your wrist bone.

Can I wear WHOOP closer to my wrist bone?

No, wearing it too close to your wrist bone can reduce accuracy and your overall WHOOP experience.

Can I wear it further away from my wrist bone (e.g., two fingers above)?

Yes, as long as it fits snugly and feels comfortable, wearing it slightly higher is perfectly fine.

Can I wear my WHOOP in the sauna?

WHOOP is designed for 24/7 wear, but we recommend avoiding extreme temperatures outside 15 - 140°F.

Can I wear my WHOOP when undergoing an MRI?

Please note that WHOOP should be treated like any other device and cannot be used near MRI machines.

What metals are used in WHOOP devices?

WHOOP 5.0 uses stainless steel and other durable, skin-safe metals in its sensor design to ensure long-term comfort and reliability. Some accessories—like our SuperKnit Luxe bands—also feature premium metals for an elevated look and feel.

How do I update my WHOOP app?

Ensure you have the latest bug fixes by updating via the App Store or Play Store. You can do this by going to your app store>searching WHOOP> and selecting update.

What is WHOOP doing to reduce waste from old devices?

We’re committed to sustainability and offer several ways to repurpose or recycle your old WHOOP hardware:

Upcycle Your WHOOP: Give your WHOOP 4.0 to a friend through our Upcycle Program and earn a $50 credit when they sign up.

Textile Recycling: Drop off your old WHOOP bands at a local textile recycling bin or send them to programs like Trashie or Suay.

Creative Reuse: Get inspired by upcycling ideas like cutting up bands for fiberboard, repurposing materials for new accessories, or sewing bands together for creative projects.

We encourage members to take advantage of these options to give their old WHOOP devices a second life and reduce waste!

Will Whoop use PFAs, perfluoro hexanoic acid, in construction of their new 5.0 bands?

WHOOP does not use materials with added PFAS in bands for WHOOP 5.0 and WHOOP MG. That said, PFAS are a large family of compounds used in many products for many years.

Unfortunately, because they do not break down naturally, some level of PFAS can now be found throughout our environment. So we cannot guarantee that our bands are free from all residual/environmental PFAS chemicals.

Does WHOOP 5.0 have magnets, a microphone, or any external buttons?

No—WHOOP 5.0 does not contain any magnets or a microphone, and it features a clean, button-free design. It’s intentionally built to be distraction-free, with no lights, sounds, or buttons—allowing you to stay focused and comfortable throughout your day.
