Apple Watch Readiness Score Explained (watchOS 27)Published onOctober 27, 2026Written byBodly Team![Apple Watch Readiness Score Explained (watchOS 27)](/_next/image?url=%2Fseo%2Fapple-watch-readiness-score.jpg&w=1920&q=75)Apple Watch Readiness is a 0 to 10 score, added in watchOS 27, that tells you how much your body can take on today: **0 to 1 Recover, 2 to 4 Pace Yourself, 5 to 7 Ready, 8 to 10 Go For It**. It combines recent activity and training load, overnight vitals (including HRV) and your sleep score. It is only on the **Series 12 and Ultra 4**, and needs at least 7 nights of sleep data before it appears.
**No Readiness on your watch?** [Bodly](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6740700700?pt=127570435&ct=apple-watch-readiness-score.bi&mt=8) gives any Apple Watch daily recovery, strain and sleep scores from the same Apple Health data, against your own baseline.
Here is what the score means, what goes into it, why it might not be showing, and what to do if your watch does not have it.

The score is relative to you. It compares your recent data with your own baseline, so two people with very different fitness can both see a 6 on a normal day.
What goes into the score
Apple groups the inputs into three factors, and the app shows which one is pulling your score up or down.

Factor
What it uses

**Activity**
Recent active calories, workout effort ratings and training load

**Vitals**
Overnight heart rate, respiratory rate, wrist temperature, HRV and blood oxygen, plus daytime heart rate

**Sleep**
Your recent [sleep score](/blog/apple-watch-sleep-score)

Two details make it different from most recovery scores:

**It updates during the day.** Calories burned, naps and daytime heart rate can move it after you wake up.
**It counts training load.** A hard week can lower your readiness even after a good night, which is closer to Garmin's Training Readiness than to WHOOP's morning-only Recovery.

Apple says the algorithm was developed with data from the Apple Heart and Movement Study.
Recovery HRV: the new number behind it
The Series 12 and Ultra 4 also add **Recovery HRV** to overnight vitals, measured against your personal baseline. Apple says HRV is now measured up to 24 times more often than before, and separates two uses: **Recovery HRV** for day-to-day stress and recovery, and **overall HRV** for longer-term cardiovascular health.
If you have used HRV on older Apple Watches, the number may look different. Older watches report HRV as SDNN, while recovery wearables such as WHOOP and Oura use RMSSD. [Apple Watch HRV](/blog/apple-watch-hrv) explains the difference, and [good HRV by age](/blog/good-hrv-by-age) shows typical values.
Why your Readiness score is not showing
Work through this list:

**Your watch model.** Readiness runs only on the Series 12 and Ultra 4. A Series 9, 10 or 11, SE 3, Ultra 2 or Ultra 3 runs watchOS 27 but does not get the app.
**Your age.** It is available for users aged 13 and over.
**Seven nights.** Set up Sleep and wear the watch to bed for at least 7 nights before the first score.
**Profile and setup.** Height, weight and age in the Fitness app on iPhone, plus Activity and Overnight Vitals set up.
**Baseline.** Vitals comparisons need you to have slept with the watch on for at least 7 of the past 49 nights.

On iPhone, Readiness is in the **Fitness app** (tap Readiness, near the bottom). On the watch, open the **Readiness app** and tap Activity, Vitals or Sleep to see what is driving the number.

Apple's design sits closest to Garmin's Training Readiness: both blend recovery with recent training. If you are comparing devices, [Apple Watch vs Garmin](/blog/apple-watch-vs-garmin) and [Garmin Body Battery explained](/blog/garmin-body-battery-explained) go deeper.
No Series 12? Getting a readiness score on an older Apple Watch
A Series 9, 10 or 11, SE 3 or Ultra 2 already records almost everything Readiness uses: overnight heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate, sleep, and on most models wrist temperature. Apple just does not turn it into a score on those watches.
Third-party apps do. They read the data from Apple Health and compute a recovery score against your own baseline. [Bodly](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6740700700?pt=127570435&ct=apple-watch-readiness-score.bi&mt=8) gives you daily strain, sleep and recovery scores plus an Energy score that rises and falls through the day, on any Apple Watch that records heart rate and sleep, along with food, weight and progress tracking. [The best Apple Watch recovery apps](/blog/best-apple-watch-recovery-apps) compares the options.

What Readiness cannot tell you
Apple is clear that Readiness does not account for everything, such as existing health conditions or medications, and should not be the only thing you rely on to decide whether it is safe to exercise. A low score when you feel fine is worth noting, not obeying blindly; a low score with symptoms such as fever, chest pain or unusual breathlessness is a reason to rest and, if it persists, to see a doctor.
