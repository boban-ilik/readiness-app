### Passage-grounded benchmark

**Apple Support 128112 (about 390 words, position 2, published October 06, 2026).** What it answers, by section. *Opening:* names the watches in one sentence ("In the Readiness app on Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Ultra 4, you can view a readiness score and a classification that helps you understand your capacity to take on activities during the day.") and never addresses other models. *Classifications:* the four bands on a 0 to 10 scale. *If you don't receive a readiness score:* four checks (height, weight and age; Activity; overnight vitals plus "worn your watch while sleeping for at least 7 nights"; Sleep enabled and "slept with your watch last night"). Model and age are not in the checklist. *How it is calculated:* three inputs with direction of effect for each, including "Increased activity in general can reduce your score, while steady or reduced activity raises it" and "Higher-than-usual heart rate variability (HRV) can lead to Favorable vitals and a high readiness score". *Disclaimer:* general wellness, not a substitute for medical judgment.
Strengths: authoritative, short, gives direction of effect for each input. Limits: no weights, no explanation of why only two models, nothing for an owner of any other watch, no mention of the 13-and-older limit (that is on the User Guide page), no statement of what an unsupported watch already shows.

**Tom's Guide (about 850 words, position 9, 2026-10-22).** What it answers: a four-step setup for owners of supported watches (check the model, enter height, weight and age, wear to bed, open the app), with screenshots, the four bands, and a note that the score "will dynamically update throughout the day". Model passage: "only those with Apple's latest S11 chip have access to the feature for now. That list includes only the Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch Ultra 4, though other models may be added down the road."
Strengths: clear ordered steps, first-hand screenshots from a wearables reviewer, mentions the watch-face widget. ## 6. Archetype recommendation

**Custom. Confidence: medium.** Runner-up: Alternatives.

- **The custom job:** a compatibility explainer with a route comparison, for one reader question: "my Apple Watch does not show Readiness; can it, why not, and what are my honest options?" Evidence standard: every Apple fact quoted from Apple's own pages with the date read; every app field from that app's US App Store listing or vendor page with the date read; Unknown shown as Unknown; no hands-on claims.
- **Evidence for Custom:** the reader's task is classification then decision, not a procedure. The only ranking page read for this segment (position 10) is itself an explainer with a table and a comparison, not steps. A How-to contract fits the dominant segment (Tom's Guide) and would push this reader's main questions into prerequisites. An Alternatives contract needs "stay or switch" guidance, and this reader cannot stay with an incumbent they do not have.
- **Evidence against Custom, for Alternatives:** the commercial heart of the piece is "what can I use instead", the vendor leads are all alternatives pages in practice, and section 7 already holds parallel rows. Why Custom still wins: without hands-on testing, a page whose main promise is ranking the alternatives would rest on store listings; the strongest sourced material is the Apple-side explanation (gaps 1, 2, 6, 7), which an Alternatives frame demotes.
- **Why not How-to, Roundup, Single review, X vs Y, Statistics:** no procedure for the unsupported reader beyond "open three apps"; a roundup or review needs testing the publisher has not done; no two-option contest; no statistics resource.
